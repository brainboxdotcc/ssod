Religion in Cryptillia has been shaped by the same things that have shaped much of its history: magic, war, demons, unexplained powers and the occasional disaster caused by someone meddling with something they probably should have left alone. There is no single faith shared across the land, and beliefs vary considerably between peoples and regions.

The Church of the Arcane Light

The Church of the Arcane Light emerged following the First Age of Magic. Its followers believe that magic is not simply a force to be exploited, but a gift from a higher power which mortals have a responsibility to use wisely.

At the centre of the faith is the belief that magical forces exist in a natural balance. Magic used to heal, create and protect is seen as an expression of that balance, while its deliberate corruption is considered an affront to it.

The clergy are known as Illuminators. Many study magic themselves, using it in healing, education and the protection of their communities. The Church has frequently opposed those who use magic to summon or empower darker forces, although arguments over precisely what constitutes an unacceptable use of magic have occupied Illuminators for almost as long as the Church has existed.

The Order of Eternal Vigilance

The Order of Eternal Vigilance owes its existence to Cryptillia's long history of demonic incursions. It is as much a military order as a religion, founded on the belief that demons will always seek a way into the mortal world and that somebody must always be prepared for their return.

Members of the Order are known as Sentinels. They train to fight creatures from beyond Cryptillia, guard places where the boundary between worlds is known to be weak, and investigate signs of demonic activity.

Their ceremonies reflect their purpose. Weapons are ritually purified before battle, old protective incantations are passed from one generation of Sentinels to the next, and considerable effort is spent maintaining wards intended to strengthen the barriers between worlds.

To a Sentinel, eternal vigilance is not merely the name of their order. Demons have returned before. They will return again.

The Seekers of the Hidden Path

The Seekers of the Hidden Path believe that much of what is understood about magic represents only a fraction of what was once known.

Ancient scrolls, forgotten ruins and magical artefacts are therefore sacred to them, not necessarily because the objects themselves deserve worship, but because of the knowledge they may contain. Their members, known as Envoys, travel throughout Cryptillia searching for surviving fragments of the past.

Some spend their lives translating a single collection of writings. Others explore ruins, recover lost artefacts or attempt to make sense of prophecies whose original authors have been dead for thousands of years.

The Seekers believe that there are deeper rules governing magic and reality which have been repeatedly discovered and forgotten throughout history. Their Hidden Path is the pursuit of those rules and, ultimately, an understanding of what lies behind them.

The Wayfarers' Fellowship

The Wayfarers' Fellowship is common amongst travellers, merchants, wanderers and those whose lives rarely keep them in one place for long.

Its followers honour the patron deity of travellers, believed to watch over those far from home and occasionally steer them away from dangers they cannot yet see. A fortunate meeting, an unexpectedly safe road or shelter found shortly before a storm may all be regarded as the quiet intervention of the Wayfarer.

There are few grand temples associated with the Fellowship. Sacred places are more often found beside roads, at crossings, mountain passes and other places generations of travellers have passed through. Small shrines are common, and travellers traditionally leave something behind before continuing on their way.

Pilgrimage is an important part of the faith, although unlike most pilgrimages, the journey matters more than the destination. A Wayfarer is expected to travel, adapt to whatever the road puts before them, and return with a few more stories than they left with.

After all, a god of travellers would have little use for followers who never went anywhere.

The Severed

Among the Dark Elves, the Severed is less a religion in the usual sense and more the keeping of a memory.

It begins with the Severance. When the races of Cryptillia gathered to establish the Peace Pact, the ships carrying their elven ancestors never arrived. Lost at sea and denied the magical protection granted to those who signed the Pact, the survivors came to believe that they had not merely been lost, but abandoned.

The Severed do not worship a god. Instead, they look to the night sky, where the stars are regarded as a record of a history that others have forgotten, ignored or deliberately buried. Their traditions preserve accounts of the Severance, the disappearance of the Night Owl emissaries and the events which divided their ancestors from the other peoples of Cryptillia.

To outsiders this can resemble prophecy or astrology, but the Severed see little distinction between studying the heavens and studying their own past. The sky was there when their ancestors were abandoned. It remains when kingdoms fall, treaties are broken and written histories are rewritten.

This belief has shaped much of Dark Elf culture. They do not seek salvation from the stars, nor expect some celestial being to put right what happened to them. What was severed cannot simply be restored by faith.

It can, however, be remembered.

And to the Severed, forgetting would be the final betrayal.
