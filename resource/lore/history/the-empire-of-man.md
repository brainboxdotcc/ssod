The Empire of Man is the largest human power in Cryptillia, ruled by a single Emperor from the imperial throne. Beneath him sits a sprawling feudal system of lords and ladies, each governing lands granted to them in the name of the Empire.

In principle, the Emperor's word is law throughout the realm. In practice, an empire is a large place.

Individual lords exercise considerable authority within their own territories. They collect taxes, maintain order, raise soldiers and administer their lands according to local laws and customs. Some rule fairly and enjoy the loyalty of their people. Others have discovered that distance from the imperial court provides considerable freedom to interpret their responsibilities.

This has made the Empire less uniform than its name might suggest. Life under one lord may bear little resemblance to life under another, and disputes between neighbouring fiefdoms are hardly unknown. So long as taxes reach the imperial treasury, soldiers answer when summoned and no lord openly challenges the throne, the Emperor has traditionally allowed a degree of local autonomy.

That arrangement has kept a vast territory governable, but it has never made it entirely stable.

Cryptillian history provides ample warning of what can happen when powerful individuals decide that what they possess is insufficient. The First Age of Magic was supposed to end with the Peace Pact, an agreement intended to establish lasting peace between the peoples of Cryptillia. Its articles were inscribed upon magically indestructible scrolls so that neither ruler, army nor the passage of time could destroy what had been agreed.

The Pact was never properly ratified.

Before that could happen, the Peace Scrolls were corrupted and transformed into the Seven Spells of Destruction. What had been created to preserve peace instead became seven of the most dangerous magical objects in Cryptillia.

The failure of the Peace Pact left the Empire, like the other realms, with no magical guarantee that the old conflicts would remain buried. Peace has endured where rulers have chosen to maintain it, but ambition, jealousy and greed were never written out of existence. Lords still feud, alliances are made and broken, and occasionally someone begins to wonder whether the person sitting on the imperial throne really deserves to be there.

For now, the Empire endures. The Emperor rules, the nobility govern in his name, taxes are collected and imperial banners continue to fly over human lands.

History suggests that none of those things should be taken for granted.
