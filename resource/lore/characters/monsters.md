Cryptillia is home to an enormous variety of creatures which its inhabitants collectively describe as monsters.

This is a convenient term rather than a useful classification.

Some are simply animals. They may be unusually large, aggressive, venomous or fond of eating travellers, but there is nothing necessarily magical about them. A creature does not need a supernatural origin to make encountering it alone in a forest a bad idea.

Others are unquestionably magical.

Centuries of spellcasting have left their mark on the living world. Some creatures have been altered by exposure to magic, while others appear to have originated through magical experiments, curses or rituals whose creators are now long dead. In many cases nobody remembers what happened, leaving scholars to argue over whether a particular creature is natural, magical or something in between.

Occasionally, somebody creates a new one.

This is generally discouraged.

There are also creatures whose origins cannot be satisfactorily explained at all. They appear in old accounts, inhabit places where few people travel, or possess abilities for which no known form of magic provides a comfortable explanation. Cryptillian scholars have produced many theories concerning such creatures.

The creatures themselves have shown little interest in resolving the debate.

Most monsters live beyond heavily populated areas. This is less because they respect civilisation and more because dangerous creatures living close to large numbers of armed people tend not to remain there for very long. Town walls, guards and regular traffic make established settlements unattractive places for large predators.

The countryside is another matter.

Human militia rarely patrol far beyond the settlements they protect, and vast areas of Cryptillia remain sparsely inhabited. Forests, mountains, ruins, caves and abandoned roads provide ample territory for creatures which would quickly be killed if they wandered through a town gate.

For most travellers, avoidance is preferable to fighting. Established roads are generally safer than unfamiliar paths, and information about dangerous areas passes readily between travellers, merchants and local communities. A warning that something has recently eaten three people near a particular bridge tends to spread quickly.

Adventurers have different priorities.

Weapons, armour and magic all have their uses, although there is no single reliable way to deal with every creature encountered in Cryptillia. An enchanted weapon may be invaluable against one monster and an unnecessarily expensive way of stabbing another perfectly ordinary animal. Protective charms and potions can improve the odds, provided the traveller knows what they are preparing for.

Wizards can repel, control or destroy some creatures through magic, but magical ability is not an answer to everything. Indeed, given the origins of certain monsters, applying additional magic to the situation can occasionally demonstrate a regrettable lack of historical awareness.

Some communities employ hunters who specialise in dealing with dangerous creatures, particularly when something begins threatening livestock or travellers. Larger settlements may offer rewards for killing a troublesome monster rather than risk their own guards pursuing it into unfamiliar country.

This arrangement has produced a steady supply of people willing to travel into dangerous places in exchange for money.

It has also produced a steady supply of work for undertakers.

Cryptillia is not covered in monsters waiting behind every tree. Most journeys are completed without anybody being eaten, poisoned, dissolved, transformed or carried away.

The possibility nevertheless encourages sensible travellers to remain on the road.

Adventurers, almost by definition, are not sensible travellers.
