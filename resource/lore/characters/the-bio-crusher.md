## The Chronicles of Desolation: Witness to the Bio Crusher

I am writing this because I saw it. At first I mistook it for a hill. There was no reason not to. It was too large to be anything else. Then it moved. Not quickly. That somehow made it worse. There was no roar, no charge, no warning. It simply advanced, and everything beneath it ceased to exist. Trees disappeared first. Not broken. Not pushed aside. Gone. I remember thinking there should have been wreckage.

Later it reached the farms. We warned them. Some fled. Some stayed. One old man refused to leave because the land had belonged to his family for six generations. The Bio Crusher crossed his farm in less time than it takes to eat supper. House. Barn. Fields. Animals. Orchard. Six generations. Nothing remained to prove any of them had ever been there. That is what people misunderstand when I tell them what I saw. The Bio Crusher does not destroy things. Fire destroys. War destroys. A fallen city leaves stone, ash, bones and graves. The Bio Crusher leaves absence.

I watched soldiers attack it. Arrows struck its surface. Wizards cast spells. Protective glyphs had been carved into the ground ahead of it. It crossed them. Perhaps they hurt it. It gave no indication that it had noticed. It did not defeat those men. To defeat somebody, you must first acknowledge that they are opposing you. The Bio Crusher simply continued.

There was a town further south. I will not write its name. Names are for places which exist. I heard every bell in the town ringing as people fled. Then one bell stopped. Then another. Then another. Then none. People ask what it wanted. I do not know. They ask whether it was angry. No. Anger would have been better. An enemy can be deceived. A beast can become full. An angry creature might be calmed. What do you offer something which does not hate you?

I followed its trail afterwards. I expected foundations, bodies, broken walls. Anything. There was almost nothing. Thousands of people had lived there. Homes, workshops, temples, records, graves, children's toys. Where did they go? Do not tell me they were destroyed. Destroyed things remain. Something remains. It has to.

Scholars argue whether the Bio Crusher is a creature, a machine or some forgotten weapon. I no longer care. Those are words we use because naming something makes us believe we understand where it belongs. I saw it. It does not belong anywhere I understand.

Sometimes I think about where it is now. While I write this, perhaps it is moving. There are people ahead of it eating supper, repairing roofs, putting children to bed, believing tomorrow matters. They do not know yet. I envy them.

I saw the Bio Crusher. I know what comes after it.

Nothing.
