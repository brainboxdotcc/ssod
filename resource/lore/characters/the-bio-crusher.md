The Chronicles of Desolation: Witness to the Bio Crusher

I am writing this because I saw it.

Others will tell you stories. They will repeat what somebody's grandfather heard from a merchant who spoke to a soldier who once saw the trail it left behind.

I saw the Bio Crusher.

I wish that distinction brought me some comfort.

At first I mistook it for a hill.

There was no reason not to. It was too large to be anything else. A great curved shape stood beyond the valley, smooth where no hill should have been smooth, its upper surface catching the morning light.

Then it moved.

Not quickly.

That somehow made it worse.

There was no charge, no roar and no moment at which the thing announced itself. It simply continued forward at the same terrible pace, and everything beneath it ceased to exist.

Trees disappeared first.

Not broken. Not pushed aside.

Gone.

I watched an entire line of forest enter beneath it. For a moment the upper branches shook as though in a storm. Then there were no branches. No trunks. No fallen timber.

There should have been wreckage.

I remember thinking that very clearly.

There should have been wreckage.

Later it reached the farms.

We had warned them.

Some believed us. Some did not. Some stood beside carts arguing over what could be carried and what must be left. One old man refused to leave because the land had belonged to his family for six generations.

Six generations.

I do not know why I keep thinking about that.

The Bio Crusher crossed his farm in less time than it takes to eat supper.

House, barn, fences, animals, fields, orchard.

Six generations.

There was nothing left with which to prove any of them had ever been there.

It does not destroy things.

That is the mistake people make when I tell them.

Fire destroys.

War destroys.

A collapsed building leaves stones. A burned forest leaves charcoal. A battlefield leaves armour, bones, broken weapons and graves.

The Bio Crusher leaves absence.

I saw people attack it.

That is another thing I wish I had not seen.

Soldiers fired arrows. Wizards cast spells against its surface. Somebody had prepared symbols in the ground, great protective marks which took twenty men most of a day to complete.

The Bio Crusher crossed them.

Nothing changed.

Perhaps the spells harmed it. Perhaps the arrows struck something important. Perhaps the glyphs inconvenienced it.

I cannot tell you.

It gave no indication that it had noticed.

That is the part I cannot explain to people.

It did not defeat them.

To defeat somebody you must first acknowledge that they are opposing you.

The Bio Crusher continued.

There was a town further south.

I will not write its name.

I have written it three times already and crossed it out three times.

Names are for places which exist.

There were bells when it approached. I could hear them from the ridge. Every bell in the town ringing at once while people poured onto the western road.

Then one tower disappeared.

Then another.

The bells stopped one at a time.

I remember that more clearly than the screaming.

One bell.

Then another.

Then another.

Then none.

It did not slow down.

People have asked what it wanted.

I do not know.

People have asked where it was going.

I do not know.

People have asked whether it was angry.

No.

That answer I can give them.

It was not angry.

Anger would have been better.

An angry thing can perhaps be calmed. An enemy can be deceived. An army can retreat. A beast can become full.

What do you offer something which does not hate you?

What do you threaten?

What do you persuade?

It crossed the town and continued towards the hills.

By sunset it was far enough away that the shape of it had begun to disappear into the haze.

I could still see the line behind it.

I followed that line for two days.

I should not have done that.

There was no reason to follow it.

I think some part of me expected to find something the distance had hidden from me. Foundations. Cellars. Bodies. Pieces of walls. Anything.

There was almost nothing.

Do you understand?

I need whoever reads this to understand.

There had been thousands of people.

Thousands.

Homes. Gardens. Workshops. Temples. Records. Children's toys. Graves. Things made by people who died hundreds of years before the Bio Crusher arrived.

Where are they?

Where did they go?

You cannot tell me they were destroyed.

Destroyed things remain.

Something remains.

It has to.

I have stopped sleeping properly.

When I close my eyes I hear the bells.

Sometimes I dream that it has already passed over this house and I simply have not noticed.

That is foolish.

I know that is foolish.

I check the floor when I wake.

There are scholars who say the Bio Crusher must be a machine.

Others call it a creature.

Some believe it was built as a weapon in an age whose history has been lost. Some insist that nothing could have constructed such a thing.

I no longer care what word they choose.

Creature.

Machine.

Weapon.

Those are names we give things because naming them makes us believe we understand where they belong.

I saw it.

It does not belong anywhere I understand.

People speak of stopping it.

I used to find courage in that.

Great warriors will be gathered. Wizards will discover some weakness. An ancient weapon will be uncovered. Somebody will do what people always do in the stories and stand before the impossible thing.

I have seen men stand before it.

It continued.

Perhaps somebody will stop it.

I hope they do.

I desperately hope they do.

But there is a thought I cannot remove from my mind.

It is moving somewhere now.

While I write this, it is moving.

While you read this, perhaps it is moving.

There are people ahead of it who are eating supper.

Someone is repairing a roof.

Someone is putting a child to bed.

Someone is arguing about money.

Someone believes tomorrow matters.

They do not know yet.

I envy them.

I envy them more than I can say.

I saw the Bio Crusher.

I know what comes after it.

Nothing.
