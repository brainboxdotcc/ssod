Demons are one of the least understood and most dangerous consequences of magic in Cryptillia.

Their existence is not a matter of religion or superstition. Demonic manifestations have been recorded for thousands of years, and possession is sufficiently well documented that even those who disagree about the nature of demons rarely dispute that they exist.

What they actually are is another matter.

Demons appear to exist without permanent physical bodies. They are often described as creatures of energy, although this is largely an attempt to give a name to something which does not behave like ordinary matter. They can manifest briefly within the physical world, but exerting sustained influence upon it usually requires a living host.

Possession is considerably more complicated than simply entering a body.

A demon must establish itself within the mind of a living creature, gradually matching and overwhelming the patterns by which that creature thinks and controls its body. This requires opportunity and is one reason demonic rituals so often involve prolonged spells, trance, exhaustion or sleep. A distracted or altered mind appears to provide the demon with the opening it needs.

Once the process is complete, there is no second occupant trapped inside.

The original mind has been overwritten.

The demon must then operate through the limitations of its new body. It must breathe, maintain a heartbeat, move muscles and process the physical senses of its host. Whatever intelligence existed before possession is gone, leaving a physical creature being operated by something which was never intended to possess a physical body at all.

Destroying that body does not necessarily destroy the demon.

This is one of the reasons experienced wizards treat possession with considerably more concern than most ordinary people. Killing the possessed may solve the immediate problem while doing nothing about the thing which possessed them.

Why demons interfere with physical life is disputed.

Many accounts describe an intense hostility towards creatures of flesh and blood. Demons appear to regard physical existence with contempt, and some have demonstrated intelligence far beyond that of the people attempting to understand them. Others behave less like invaders and more like predators, opportunists or simply things pursuing purposes which make little sense to a mortal observer.

It is entirely possible that calling all of them "demons" says more about Cryptillian understanding than it does about the creatures themselves.

Amongst the stranger attempts to explain their behaviour is a theory known as the Thermaturgical Divide.

Its proponents argue that magic does not merely draw upon an otherwise empty source of power. The unseen forces manipulated by a spell may form part of an environment inhabited by demons. Every spell cast into the world disturbs that environment, and sufficiently extensive use of magic may attract attention from whatever lives within it.

If true, the implications are unpleasant.

Magic is woven throughout Cryptillian civilisation. It heals the sick, protects settlements, moves goods across enormous distances and performs tasks upon which entire communities depend. Simply abandoning it would be neither practical nor, in many places, survivable.

Yet the Thermaturgical Divide suggests that the more heavily mortals use magic, the more they interfere with something on the other side.

Most magical scholars reject the theory.

A few reject it rather nervously.

Knowledge of possession itself is similarly confused. Popular belief often holds that a victim must somehow invite a demon inside, or that possession demonstrates weakness of character. Evidence accumulated by those who study the phenomenon suggests that matters are nowhere near so simple.

Those who discover too much have an unfortunate tendency not to remain available to explain what they discovered.

There are also accounts of entities which appear to exist far beyond the scale of ordinary demons.

One of the oldest is known only as That Which Hungers.

References to it are rare, inconsistent and separated by enormous spans of time. It is unclear whether That Which Hungers is a demon, something from which lesser demons originated, or an entirely different form of intelligence which happens to inhabit the same unseen realm.

Descriptions agree on remarkably little except its age and its appetite.

It does not appear to think on the scale of an individual mortal life. Where lesser demons manipulate people, That Which Hungers is associated with events affecting entire peoples and regions. Some surviving writings imply that encounters remembered as separate demonic events may instead have been different expressions of the same intelligence.

One particularly old warning concerning it has survived in several forms.

It remembers.

The greatest danger posed by demons may therefore not be that Cryptillia is awaiting some future invasion.

It may be that every spell ever cast has been reaching into a place which was already occupied.
