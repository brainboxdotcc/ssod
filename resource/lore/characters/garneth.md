For almost fifty years, the southern reaches of Utopia have lived with the influence of a creature known as Demon Garneth.

The name is not entirely accurate.

Garneth was once a Human lord, ruling a considerable territory in southern Utopia from his castle. Contemporary accounts describe an ambitious man with an appetite for greater power and territory, although precisely what happened to him afterwards remains uncertain.

At some point, Garneth became possessed.

Whether this happened through his own actions, through an attempt to obtain greater magical power, or through circumstances entirely beyond his control is unknown. Much of what survives from the period consists of rumour, second-hand accounts and conclusions drawn after the event.

Whatever entered Lord Garneth did not leave.

The distinction between the man and the thing possessing him has long since become largely academic. For most inhabitants of Utopia, "Garneth" now means the demon.

No reliable witness has seen Garneth himself for decades.

He remains within his castle, a place which has been effectively lost to ordinary Human control. Attempts to determine what occurs inside have produced little useful information, and those inclined to investigate personally have generally found easier ways to shorten their lives.

His isolation should not be mistaken for inactivity.

Creatures and servants associated with Garneth have been encountered throughout southern Utopia. His influence extends far beyond the castle itself, with reports ranging from the vicinity of Larret Bridge in the north to the approaches to Port Obligan in the south.

Exactly how much control Garneth exercises over these creatures is uncertain. Some clearly act in his interests. Others may merely have flourished in territory made increasingly dangerous by his presence.

After half a century, people have adapted.

Roads have changed. Settlements know which areas to avoid. Merchants exchange warnings about recent activity. Travellers learn that a shorter route through Garneth's territory is not necessarily a faster one.

This does not mean the entire south has been abandoned. People still live, trade and travel there. Port Obligan remains an important settlement, and life continues surprisingly close to places which outsiders might consider intolerably dangerous.

People can become accustomed to almost anything if it lasts long enough.

Successive rulers, soldiers, adventurers and magicians have considered the problem of Garneth. None has produced a permanent solution.

Attacking the castle directly presents the obvious difficulty of reaching it, followed by the considerably greater difficulty of entering it, followed by whatever difficulties Garneth has arranged for anyone fortunate enough to accomplish both.

There is also the uncomfortable problem of not knowing precisely what waits inside.

Demonic possession is poorly understood even by accomplished wizards. Garneth has remained possessed for approximately half a century, far longer than most documented cases survive without either the host or those around them being destroyed.

What this means is disputed.

Some believe the demon has simply found an unusually useful host. Others believe Garneth has become something substantially more dangerous than an ordinary possessed Human.

There are people who claim to know more.

They tend not to explain how.

For the inhabitants of southern Utopia, such arguments matter rather less than the practical reality. There is a castle they do not approach, a former lord who has not been seen for decades, and things wandering the countryside which are best avoided.

Lord Garneth disappeared almost fifty years ago.

Demon Garneth has been there ever since.
