Barbarians are an ancient offshoot of Humanity who spent thousands of years completely isolated from the other peoples of Cryptillia.

They inhabit the far northern wastes, beyond regions which most Humans historically considered worth exploring. Cut off from their southern relatives for millennia, they developed their own culture, beliefs and technology, with no knowledge of the kingdoms and empires which had risen and fallen elsewhere in the world.

For most of recorded history, the rest of Cryptillia had equally little idea that the Barbarians existed.

That changed less than a century ago.

Dwarven explorers pushing further into the far north than previous expeditions encountered settlements belonging to a people who were unmistakably Human in origin, yet belonged to no Human civilisation known to them. What had begun as another Dwarven expedition had unexpectedly become first contact with an entire people.

The Dwarves may have possessed more advanced technology, but the Barbarians possessed something considerably more useful in the far north: experience.

Barbarians taught the newcomers how to survive winters in which the sun could disappear for months, how to find food and shelter when the landscape froze, and how to hunt animals the Dwarves had previously known only as unfamiliar shapes moving across the ice. Their people had survived these conditions for thousands of years. To them, the northern wastes were not an unexplored frontier. They were home.

The Dwarves brought different knowledge in return.

Dwarven tools and machinery introduced technologies which had developed elsewhere during the Barbarians' long isolation. More importantly, Dwarven transport offered something the Barbarians had never possessed: a practical route out of the far north.

For the first time in thousands of years, Barbarians began travelling south.

The Human Empire became a particular source of fascination. To the Barbarians it was simultaneously familiar and completely alien: an enormous civilisation populated by people who were recognisably related to them, but whose culture, government and way of life had developed independently for millennia.

Their isolation also meant that Barbarians had inherited almost none of Cryptillia's traditional rivalries. Elves, Orcs and the Kin were not ancient enemies or allies. Until comparatively recently, they were peoples the Barbarians did not know existed.

Dwarves were the exception. They were the first outsiders the Barbarians encountered and the people through whom much of their early contact with the wider world took place. The relationship began not with conquest, but with an exchange of things each side possessed and the other needed.

As a result, Barbarians generally approach outsiders without many of the inherited suspicions common elsewhere in Cryptillia. They are often curious, willing to trade and prepared to negotiate, although generations raised without knowledge of southern customs inevitably produce the occasional misunderstanding.

Their openness should not be mistaken for helplessness or naivety. The Dwarves arrived with machinery, ships and knowledge gathered from across Cryptillia.

The Barbarians were the ones who knew how not to freeze to death.

Today, fewer than a hundred years separate the Barbarians from their first contact with the wider world. Some remain in the north and have little interest in leaving. Others travel increasingly far from home, returning with foreign technology, unfamiliar ideas and stories of places their grandparents did not know existed.

For the rest of Cryptillia, the Barbarians are an ancient people.

For the Barbarians, most of Cryptillia is new.
