Dwarves are considerably less numerous than Humans, but they have a habit of turning up almost anywhere in Cryptillia. Exploration comes naturally to them, and few places are considered too distant, inaccessible or dangerous if there is something worth finding there.

To a Dwarf, wealth is not limited to gold.

Material wealth is certainly important. Gold, precious stones, rare materials, finely crafted objects and other valuables may remain within a family for centuries. But knowledge is wealth too. A new method of working metal, a map of an unexplored cave system, the design of an unusual machine or simply knowing something that nobody else knows can be every bit as valuable.

This attitude has driven Dwarven exploration for generations. A Dwarf returning from a journey with little gold but knowledge of a new mineral deposit, an unknown land or a better way to build something has not returned empty-handed.

Perhaps surprisingly to those familiar only with their reputation as miners, Dwarves have a strong affinity with water. They are accomplished boat builders and sailors, and Dwarven vessels are a common sight on the rivers and seas of Cryptillia. Rivers provide routes deep into otherwise difficult country, while the sea offers access to distant lands, unfamiliar peoples and, most importantly, things worth bringing home.

Dwarves have consequently played an important part in trade between distant parts of Cryptillia. A cargo of ore, tools or manufactured goods may travel outwards and return as gold, unusual materials, foreign craftsmanship or information. To a Dwarf, a profitable voyage need not necessarily be measured by the weight of the purse at the end of it.

Their relationship with water has influenced their technology as much as mining has. Dwarven engineering can be found in ships, docks, locks, pumps and other machinery associated with moving people, water and cargo. A Dwarf is just as likely to be found examining the workings of a harbour as digging beneath a mountain.

It has also made Dwarves amongst the most technologically advanced peoples of Cryptillia. Knowledge is accumulated, protected and passed down in much the same way as material wealth. Techniques discovered generations ago are refined rather than forgotten, while explorers and craftspeople continually add to what is already known. Dwarven expertise is particularly advanced in mining, metalworking and machinery, and they have even achieved limited forms of mechanical flight.

Humans may be more numerous, but Dwarves have spent centuries treating useful knowledge as something worth acquiring and keeping.

Family wealth therefore consists of considerably more than a pile of gold. A Dwarven inheritance might include precious metals alongside tools, designs, maps, journals, techniques and secrets accumulated over generations. Its value lies as much in what the family knows as in what it owns.

Consequently, attempting to steal from a Dwarven family is unwise.

A Dwarf may appear to value the family wealth above almost anything else, sometimes even above members of their own family. Outsiders occasionally find this difficult to understand. A family member, after all, is expected to be capable of defending themselves.

The family hoard cannot pick up an axe.

When that hoard is threatened, Dwarves who might otherwise disagree about almost everything have an impressive ability to remember that they are related.

Once a Dwarf leaves home to seek wealth of their own, they are often fiercely independent. Many prefer to travel alone, choosing their own direction and taking as much time as necessary to investigate whatever catches their interest. Some seek precious materials. Others seek forgotten places, unusual technology or knowledge which has not yet found its way into Dwarven hands.

For many, a river or coastline is therefore an invitation rather than a boundary. If it leads somewhere unfamiliar, somebody will eventually build a boat and find out where.

Some eventually return home with enough wealth to add considerably to that of their family. Others establish homes, workshops and hoards of their own. A few simply continue travelling.

For a Dwarf, there is always something else worth knowing.
