Goblins occupy an unusual position within Orcish society. They are neither as physically powerful as Orcs nor subjected to the outright slavery imposed upon Lesser Orcs. Instead, their place beneath the Orcs is maintained largely through culture.

Physically, a Goblin is not particularly remarkable by Cryptillian standards. They are generally of a stocky build, closer to Humans than Orcs and without the enormous musculature associated with their larger cousins. In a society which treats physical strength as one of the clearest measures of worth, this immediately places them at a disadvantage.

Mentally, the situation is rather different.

Goblins are cunning, observant and often considerably better than Orcs at planning. They have the patience for problems which cannot be solved by hitting them, and much of the organisation required to keep an Orcish tribe functioning inevitably falls to Goblins.

Orcish culture nevertheless treats them as inferior.

A Goblin who fails confirms what everybody already knew about Goblins. A Goblin who succeeds has done surprisingly well, for a Goblin. One who becomes too confident will soon encounter an Orc willing to remind them where they stand.

This attitude is repeated throughout their lives. Goblins are told what they are good for, what they are incapable of and why positions of genuine authority properly belong to Orcs. Their intelligence may be useful, but it does not make them equal.

After generations of this, many Goblins believe it themselves.

"How could we ever expect more?" is an attitude found as readily amongst Goblins as amongst the Orcs who benefit from it. A Goblin may complain endlessly about an incompetent Orc superior while never seriously considering that the superior's position could have been theirs.

This makes Goblin subordination considerably different from the slavery of Lesser Orcs. An Orc does not normally need to chain a Goblin to make them remain in their place. The expectations of the society around them have already drawn the boundaries.

Goblins perform many of the practical jobs within Orcish settlements. They tend animals, organise supplies, prepare food, maintain equipment and undertake the countless ordinary tasks without which a tribe of warriors would very quickly discover that being good at fighting does not make dinner appear.

Boars are commonly kept by Goblins, while captured horses which are not retained for other purposes frequently end their lives under Goblin knives before becoming part of an Orcish battle-feast.

In combat, Goblins rely less upon strength and more upon speed and an irritating unwillingness to remain where an opponent swings a weapon. They use a more limited range of weapons than Orc warriors, but their agility makes them difficult targets and particularly troublesome when several work together.

The greatest limitation placed upon Goblins may therefore not be physical at all.

A Goblin who genuinely stops believing that Orcs are supposed to be above them can become a surprisingly difficult problem for the Orcs.

Fortunately for the Orcs, their society has spent generations teaching Goblins not to have such ideas.
